  <section id="method" class="method-section" aria-labelledby="method-title"><div class="wrap section"><div class="method-heading"><p class="eyebrow">02 / HOW I WORK</p><h2 id="method-title">Make the comparison<br>worth trusting.</h2><p>A result is useful when its data choices, evaluation rules and limits are visible.</p></div><div class="method-steps"><article><span class="step-no">01</span><div><h3>Respect the clock.</h3><p>Train on target dates through 2015, then test on future origins. Build lags on a complete calendar and exclude boundary-crossing samples. No future measured weather enters the predictors.</p></div></article><article><span class="step-no">02</span><div><h3>Keep the difficult cases visible.</h3><p>Compare all-day error with high-case error, recall and precision. Fix the high cutoff using training data and use identical test station-days for every model.</p></div></article><article><span class="step-no">03</span><div><h3>Show uncertainty. Share the steps.</h3><p>Use paired seven-day block resamples, keeping stations from each date together. Publish dependency versions, source attribution, calculation code and readable SVG figures.</p></div></article></div></div></section>
  <section class="section wrap limits" aria-labelledby="limits-title"><div><p class="eyebrow">03 / THE LIMITS MATTER</p><h2 id="limits-title">A historical benchmark.<br>A starting point.</h2></div><div><p>These results describe twelve Beijing stations in 2013–2017. They do not establish present-day performance, population exposure or the causes of pollution.</p><p>Missing observations, the chosen cutoff and the test period affect the comparison. Bootstrap intervals condition on the fitted models and do not capture training uncertainty.</p><p class="next-step"><b>Next:</b> inspect station-level errors, evaluate rolling forecast origins and repeat the study on more recent data.</p></div></section>
